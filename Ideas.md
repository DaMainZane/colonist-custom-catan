5. Warlords core.
6. Wild Earth.
7. Guildhall.
8. The social layer (diplomacy, nemesis, minigames).
9. Scenario presets.
10. Real-time mode, then Dominion mode last.

## Dominion Mode

- [ ] General - **Dominion mode (unit mode)**: i want a gamemode which serve as the main reason why dlcs exist (of course, dlc can be play without this mode). It is unit mode. Where it is a combination of risk domination, catan, and battle of polytopia. engine of resources is catan, unit cost and creation is linked to catan resources. The unit variety themselves are inspired from battle of polytopia. The movement is also from them (with roads already built in). but the mechanic is similar to risk domination. the dlcs mechanics of the world gen is to complement the units. But the player can still play with dlcs world gen if they wish to without units. Need to refine this [+brainstorm: working name Dominion is a placeholder. CORE IDEA: the abstract versions already on this board (garrison tokens from Knights as armies, Mercenaries, wave strength, caravan tokens) are the no-unit fallback of this mode; units and the abstract version share the same strength and defense numbers, so each DLC addon is built once and works both ways. TURN: roll, produce, trade and build (including recruiting), then a command phase (each unit moves and acts once), then end turn; Catan's economy is untouched and units are just another thing you buy. TERRITORY: units stand on hexes (not vertices), which fits Risk-style territories, terrain, fog and walls (walls already enclose hexes); buildings stay on corners and are attacked from the hexes around them; regions (landmasses, biome clusters, wall zones) act like Risk continents but holding one pays a resource bonus, not free troops, so units stay tied to Catan resources. UNITS (placeholder costs, tune in sims): Warrior 1 wheat + 1 sheep (strength 1); Archer 1 wood + 1 wheat (hits one hex away, weak when attacked); Rider 1 sheep + 1 wheat + 1 wood (moves 2); Defender 1 brick + 1 wheat (defense bonus, holds walls); Swordsman 1 ore + 1 wheat + 1 sheep (strength 2); Knight 2 ore + 1 wheat + 1 sheep (strength 3, moves 2, the Knight dev card becomes a free recruit); Catapult 2 wood + 1 ore + 1 brick (breaks walls and castles, slow); Boat wood + sheep (Tidebound, carries a unit across water); Cloak 1 sheep + 1 ore (Diplomacy tech, stealth, one sabotage); Mind Bender gem cost (late tech, converts an enemy unit). # Integration Layer (added 2026-10-26)

This section comes from a pass over every idea above, checking how each one connects to the rest of the game. Existing idea text was left untouched. Everything new is below, so it can be reviewed, accepted or deleted as one block.

## Design principle: the Rule of Two

- Every addon needs (1) a real use case on its own, meaning a decision a player makes differently because it exists, and (2) at least two interconnections.
- An interconnection is one of four kinds: **Mechanic** (reads or writes a shared variable), **UI** (reuses a shared component), **Content** (registers cards, events, techs, contract conditions, stats or dialogue lines into a shared list), **Bot** (feeds a bot goal, threat or dialogue trigger).
- At least one of the two links must go to an always-on General service, so the addon still works when every other DLC is off. Cross-DLC links are soft links: they exist only when both DLCs are on, and each is listed in the pair matrix below. A DLC may depend on General, never hard-depend on another DLC (existing exception: Siege needs Bulwark, Rising sea needs Tidebound, which the lobby prompts for).
- Enforce it in code: each addon manifest entry carries a `links` list (service or addon id plus kind). A test fails the build if an addon has fewer than 2 links or none to a General service. Also useful as the data source for the lobby synergy chips (see New ideas, General).
- New addon checklist: manifest entry with links; at least one event, contract condition, tech, stat or dialogue line when it fits; per-tier bot behavior; a no-unit form and a unit-mode form if it touches strength.

## Shared services (build once, every DLC plugs in)

1. **DLC manifest and registries**: one entry per DLC and per addon (id, name, dependencies, links) plus registries for events, tech branches, contract conditions, stats, dialogue lines, placement types, tile states and bot goals. The lobby DLC cards are generated from it.
2. **Round clock**: one scheduler that every periodic effect subscribes to (Tides, Seasons, Wall decay, Plague spread, waves, War weariness, Debt due dates, fate tokens, Hat day). A round is "everyone has gone once" in turn mode and N seconds in Real-time mode, so Real-time Catan becomes a config switch and not a rewrite.
3. **Score abstraction**: `score()` returns public VP in VP mode and total produced in Sandbox economy. Taxes, Catch-up, zone control, leaderCutoff, Drunk robber ownership and contract rewards all read it, never raw VP. `publicVP()` is its VP-mode implementation.
4. **Piece cost and limit service**: base cap, the "double cost above base" rule, per-piece-type limits. Used by bridges, walls, castles, harbors, nets, gates, landmarks, and by the Shipwreck floor rule (payout is at least the claimer's build cost plus 1).
5. **Trade ratio function**: one function with a 2:1 floor (gem ports excepted). Used by ports, Harbor upgrade, Market landmark, tech tree, Sheep economy, War weariness, Embassies and the invalid trade ratio prompt.
6. **Production pipeline with a fixed modifier order**: roll, season number shift, tile state (flooded, barren, ash, plagued), base yield, castle multiplier, wild token conversion, tax, bank-short handling, discard limit. Every addon that touches production declares where it sits in this order.
7. **Tile state layer**: per-tile status with timers (flooded, drowned, silt, ash, fertile, barren, crater, plagued, oasis, enclosed zone id). Writers: Tides, Rising sea, Volcano, Meteor, Plague, Seasons, Ground Work, Walls. One reader: the production pipeline.
8. **Durability and repair**: one durability value and one repair action for palisades, stone, siege walls, rickety bridges, nets and gates.
9. **Strength and defense model**: garrison tokens, mercenaries, wave strength, wall tier, building base defense (1/2/3), Largest Army tiebreak. The same numbers work with no units and with Dominion units.
10. **Marker system**: a world marker has an owner, a countdown and a payload. Used by wrecks, pirate wrecks, waves, siege tokens, ruins loot, caravans, occupied tokens, craters. Same icon frame and timer ring for all.
11. **Overlay and popup kit**: tint layer (pattern as well as color) for incomplete wall, flood, plague, wave, crack, season shift; timer-bar popup (trade, peace offer, veto, permit); event title card (overthrown, betrayal, eruption); round-end recap card.
12. **Wild token**: the gold-pick flow generalized. Fish, Oasis and any future any-one-resource token spend through it and are ignored by bank cap and the "no one has x-resource" trade check.
13. **Ledger**: one record shape (who, to whom, amount, due round) for debts, taxes, fate tokens, gate permits, pacts, weariness counters and tavern bets.
14. **Relations matrix**: per-pair state (war, neutral, peace, alliance) plus bot opinions. Read by trade UI, robber and steal rules, wall passage, embassies, caravans, instigation, nemesis and bot trade leeway.
15. **Public history ring buffer, change log and stats registry**: rolls, robber targets, production, claims, coups, wrecks. Feeds the drinking game, instigation, nemesis, recap card, hat unlocks, contract conditions and the victory screen.
16. **Seeded event deck**: base cards plus each DLC's cards, preview and fate token flow, deterministic for replays.
17. **Seven Resolver**: an ordered pipeline for everything that fires on a 7 (see Conflicts below).
18. **Bot goal registry**: each addon registers goal valuation, threat map input, tier gating and dialogue lines with needs-tags.
19. **Persistence**: data/bots and data/players on a persistent volume, hard caps, migration.
20. **Sim matrix**: scenarios x DLC pairs x bot tiers, producing the bot-tested badge.

## Conflicts to resolve before building

- **Seven pile-up.** Four DLCs hook a 7: Rickety bridges collapse, Wall decay, Volcano destruction, Karaoke 7, on top of the base discard and robber. Define the Seven Resolver order: discard, karaoke, robber move and steal, volcano, decay and collapse. Add a setting for how many DLC side effects may fire on one 7 (default 2, picked by priority) so a 7 does not wipe the board.
- **Seasons versus red-number rules.** Shifting numbers can put two red numbers (6 and 8) next to each other. Validate after each shift (or shift only within safe values), and make bot pip scoring read the current number, never the original.
- **Rickety bridges and Longest Road.** Decide if bridges count. If they do, a collapse recalculates Longest Road mid-turn and must go through the change log.
- **Sheep economy and wild token.** If sheep are "currency", define whether sheep count as any one resource (that breaks balance) or only pay specific things (debt interest, market fees, contract fees, caravan cargo). This document assumes the second.
- **Sandbox and Real-time versus VP features.** Taxes, Catch-up, contracts, zone control and monuments all assume VP. Route them through `score()` and let each mode define what a contract pays.
- **Robber placement versus tile states.** The robber cannot sit on flooded or drowned tiles. Decide what happens to a robber standing on a tile that floods (moves to the nearest dry tile, owner of the last knight chooses).

- **Debt**: Mercenaries on credit; Credit crunch event; Famine loans offered by the bank after a drought or plague; Banking tech lowers interest; Catch-up gives trailing seats cheaper rates; contract condition: clear a debt; interest runs on the round clock; all entries are public ledger lines.
- **Contracts** (as a General service): each DLC adds a condition pack, so the contract board is how players get pulled toward DLC content; contracts can be resolved by instant or cycle mode; Sandbox mode pays resources instead of VP.

**Warlords**
- **Embassies** (merged with Diplomacy T2): a standing gate permit and caravan pass; pays a small shared benefit each round; immune to Debt seizure; exempt from War weariness; reveals the area around the host settlement (Polytopia-style).
- **Siege** (define for no-unit mode first): enemy strength adjacent to a wall for a full round compares against wall durability plus garrison; Wall decay and repair are the defender's levers; Catapult is the unit-mode version; Gatehouse can be forced open by a successful siege.
- **Wave defense (co-op)**: waves are announced through the event preview; targets are weighted toward harbors and coast castles; defeated waves drop wrecks; co-op score uses `score()` so it also works in Sandbox.
- **King vs peasant**: the king's gate is the peasants' entry point (Gatehouse); a public contract "overthrow the king" pays VP; peasants get Catch-up levers (cheaper techs, merc discount); the king seat can be a castle; Kingdoms scenario preset.
- **Player vs AI warlord**: the warlord is a persistent named bot with nemesis memory, so it can remember who beat it last time and return with a title; it uses the Overload doctrine and strength model, with no separate combat engine.

**General**
- **Catch-up**: a single rubber-band lever menu (Off / Light / Strong) that every DLC can add levers to: higher discard limit, discount on the first tech, cheaper loan rate, free merc slot, pity card from the drinking game, regained fate token. The host chooses an intensity, not individual switches.
- **Sandbox economy**: needs `score()` abstraction; tech tree matters more with no VP; contracts pay resources; bots get a production-maximizer goal; end-game stats feed hat unlocks.
- **Real-time Catan**: only works if everything periodic is on the round clock; Events, Tides and Seasons keep their cadence in seconds; popups and minigames need longer timers or auto-skip; good fit for vs AI and Sandbox first.

## Suggested order of build (assuming every idea is fully specced)

Principle: build services first, then the DLC with the fewest outside dependencies, then DLCs in the order their prerequisites appear. Each stage ends with a sim-matrix gate before the next starts.

**Stage 0: Foundations (no player-facing DLC yet)**
1. DLC manifest, registries, and the UI rework of the intermediary page (needed to host DLC cards and presets).
2. Round clock and `score()` / `publicVP()`.
3. Piece cost and limit service, trade ratio function, production pipeline, tile state layer, wild token, durability and repair.
4. Overlay and popup kit, marker system, seeded event deck, Seven Resolver skeleton.
5. Public history ring buffer, change log, stats registry; bot memory folder with persistence checked in render.yaml; bot goal registry; sim matrix harness; link audit test.
- Gate: base game plays unchanged with all services in place and nothing registered.

**Stage 1: General features that DLCs ride on**
6. Events plus preview and fate tokens; Public vs hidden VP; Taxes and Catch-up with the lever menu.
7. Public contract board plus rarity tiers (base conditions only); Spies dev card; strength and defense model (garrison tokens, base defense).
8. Tech tree skeleton with Tech costs by empire size (base branch only); Round-end recap card; local Nemesis ledger.
9. Cheap proving-ground DLC content: Bot drinking game, Hat day and Karaoke 7 (they exercise the history buffer, dialogue registry, event deck and Seven Resolver at low risk).
10. Sandbox economy (cheap mode that proves the `score()` abstraction).
- Gate: every General service has at least two consumers.

**Stage 2: Tidebound**
11. Bridges, Rickety bridges, Tides plus Flood warnings, Harbor upgrade, storm event plus Shipwreck salvage, Fishing, Tidebound tech branch.
- Why first: fewest outside dependencies, and it gives the overlay layer, wild token, marker system and ratio function their first real consumers.

**Stage 3: Bulwark (core)**
12. Walls, zone control and tax, Wall tiers with durability, Castle, Wall decay, Gatehouse, Sea wall, Drawbridge.
- Gate: wall zones, gate state and sea wall pass sims with Tidebound on and off.

**Stage 4: Warlords (core, no relations yet)**
13. Knights as armies, Mercenaries, War mechanic (war and neutral only), Embassies merged with Diplomacy T2 as a stub.
14. Bulwark Siege and Castle sieges (now that strength exists); Wave defense (co-op) and Risk-lite; King vs peasant; Player vs AI warlord; Tidebound Pirates and Blockade.
- Gate: Kingdoms scenario passes bot sims.

**Stage 5: Wild Earth**
15. Volcano, Meteor crater, Aftermath tiles, Silt, Biome tags, Seasons, Plague plus Quarantine card, Quarantine gate, Plague doctor tech, Plague attrition, Almanac, Oasis.
16. Emerald and diamond tiles plus Gem market (needs the port rework and Harbor), Obsidian vein, Plague by sea.

**Stage 6: Guildhall**
17. Landmarks (placement registry), Landmark set bonus, Ruins, Granary, contract condition packs, Monuments, Lighthouse tide sight.
18. Sheep economy, Debt, Debt tie-ins (needs Embassies), Famine loans, Mercenaries on credit, Trade caravans (needs bridges, gates, embassies), Sea caravans, Toll.

**Stage 7: Social layer**
19. Full relations matrix (peace and alliance), Alliances and betrayal, Diplomacy branch, War weariness, Capital vision, Intel ladder.
20. Persistent Nemesis layer, Instigation, Hat unlocks and DLC hat pools, Hat of shame.
21. Duel registry, robbery minigames, Tavern bets, Drunk robber, Last call.

**Stage 8: Scenarios and balance**
22. Scenario presets (Kingdoms, Drowned World, Party Night, Merchant Republic, Cataclysm, Everything Bagel); run all 15 DLC pairs through the sim matrix; lobby synergy chips; bot-tested badges.

**Stage 9: Heavy modes**

24. Unit Mode Design.md (settle the five open decisions first), then Dominion mode: units engine, hex control, combat, capture, DLC hooks, unit-mode bot addon. Last because it reuses the strength numbers and every DLC hook built above.
